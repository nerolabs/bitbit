Silt — Genesis
================

This is the first file on every Silt network: the block from which
all others descend, carried by every node, hosted by the whole swarm,
retrievable by anyone, forever. It holds no secret. It is here to say
what this network is for.

    The infrastructure is not the content.

A Silt node stores and serves anonymous, encrypted fragments. It
cannot read what it holds; it was built, deliberately, to be unable to.
Chunks are ciphertext. Manifests are ciphertext describing ciphertext.
A file's public name is thirty-two bytes of hash that reveal nothing —
not a title, not a size, not a kind. The network moves bytes the way a
road moves traffic and a wire moves current: faithfully, and without
opinion about the cargo.

Meaning lives elsewhere, with whoever holds the keys. To open a file
you need a link, and a link is a key, and keys travel between people —
not through this network. What a thing IS, whether it may be shared,
who is responsible for it: those are questions for the layer above,
where names attach to numbers and humans attach to names. This layer
answers a smaller, humbler question — is the data still here? — and
answers it well: split across many machines, healed as they fail,
provably held, and gone the instant its holders are gone and no one
kept a key.

We built it this way on purpose. A network that cannot know what it
carries cannot be made to lie about it, cannot be conscripted to
censor it, cannot be blamed for it any more than the alphabet is
blamed for what is written. Privacy here is not a setting. It is the
shape of the thing.

To whoever runs a node: thank you. You are giving the network storage
and bandwidth, and asking nothing of the files but that they be served
honestly. To whoever reads this from a fresh clone: everything after
this block was earned — audited, attested, agreed. This block was
simply declared, once, so the rest would have somewhere to begin.

Build something worth carrying.

— The first block. Held by all. Owned by none.
